How to read this

  • A pattern is a fixed rule written down before the games it is tested on. Patterns 1 to 5 were frozen on October 9, pattern 6 on the morning of October 10. The rules do not change; a changed rule is a new study.
  • The discovery column is the record on the games that suggested each pattern. It is shown so you can see where the idea came from and it counts for nothing. The same market produced those numbers and will regress toward its long-run rates, which for spreads and totals is about 50%.
  • The graded line is the verified closing consensus line which is what you could have possibly received late as a bettor. It is not necessarily the best price of the week. The next table is what the best retail price was at the time of the selection freeze. It is not the line you will be graded on.
  • Moneyline favorites in patterns 1 and 5 are expensive. Two upsets undo six or seven wins. The spread and total patterns risk one unit to win 0.91.
  • A verdict comes once, after the regular season, and only for patterns that reach their sample floor. Anything short of the floor is described as what it is: too few games.
Source: a private ledger of college football closing lines captured from retail and offshore books, graded against official final scores. Prices and lines are observations, not offers. This page is updated weekly and after each Saturday's games.
